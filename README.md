# HRB_sub
Species-level gut microbial profiles from two cohorts for diabetes and chronic kidney disease

## Project overview
This project is based on two analytical subcohorts of the Harbin Health Biobank, an ongoing population-based screening program launched in Harbin, China, in April 2024 and scheduled to continue through December 2026. Participants included in HRB_sub1 and HRB_sub2 were enrolled in 2024 and were aged 45–64 years at enrollment. The broader project aims to characterize major cardiometabolic and renal risk factors and their associated gut microbial alterations. The two analytical subcohorts described here were used specifically to assess associations between gut microbial species and diabetes or chronic kidney disease (CKD).
## Study cohorts
HRB_sub1 included 2,786 participants, comprising 1,100 participants with diabetes and 1,686 age- and sex-matched controls without diabetes. Diabetes was defined as fasting plasma glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L, HbA1c ≥6.5%, or self-reported physician-diagnosed type 2 diabetes. HRB_sub2 included 1,245 participants, comprising 249 participants with CKD and 996 age- and sex-matched controls without CKD. CKD was defined as an estimated glomerular filtration rate <60 ml/min/1.73 m² or self-reported physician-diagnosed CKD.
## Metagenomic profiling
Fecal samples collected at enrollment underwent shotgun metagenomic sequencing (MGI, Shenzhen). After sequence quality control and removal of host reads, species-level relative abundance profiles were generated using MetaPhlAn4 (version 4.1.0; database mpa_vOct22_CHOCOPhlAnSGB_202212).
## Data included in this record
This record provides the deidentified analysis data used for analyses, including species-level relative abundance profiles for HRB_sub1 and HRB_sub2 and sample-level metadata comprising sample identifiers, age, sex, smoking status, alcohol drinking status, fruit and vegetable intake, medication use and diabetes or CKD status. A README describes the file structure, variable definitions, and the filtering and data transformation procedures used in the analyses. No raw sequencing reads, direct participant identifiers, or additional clinical data are included.
## Ethics approval
All participants provided written informed consent, and the study was approved by the Ethics Committee of BGI (no. BGI-IRB 24037) and the Medical Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University (no. KY2025-042).
